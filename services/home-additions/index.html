Home Additions in the GTA

Home Services Home Additions

Quick answer: a home addition in the GTA typically runs $60,000 to $450,000 depending on size and type. A small bump-out starts around $60,000. A full second-storey addition can pass $250,000. Permit timelines, not construction, are usually what stretches the schedule.

Moving is expensive, disruptive, and doesn't actually fix the problem if the only thing wrong with your house is that it's too small for how your family lives now. A home addition solves that without leaving the neighbourhood, the school district, or the mortgage rate you already have.

An addition is a bigger commitment than most renovations, because it changes your home's footprint, not just what's inside it. That means structural engineering, permits, and zoning review are part of the job from day one, not an afterthought.

What a Home Addition Actually Involves

Every addition starts with a zoning and setback review, since your lot determines what's actually buildable before any design work is worth doing. From there, the scope typically covers: structural engineering and permit drawings, foundation work matched to the addition type, whether that's a slab, crawlspace, or full basement extension, framing tied into your existing roofline, exterior finishes matched to your home's siding or brick, electrical panel capacity and HVAC extension to heat and cool the new space, and full interior build-out once the shell is weathertight.

Illustrative Cost Ranges

Home addition cost scales with footprint and whether you're building out or up. Here's what each tier generally includes.

Bump-Out Addition
$60K–$110K

Roughly 100-200 sq ft, extended kitchen, mudroom, or breakfast nook, minimal foundation work.

Single-Storey Addition

300-500 sq ft, one or two new rooms, full foundation, roofline tied into the existing structure.

Second-Storey Addition
$250K–$450K+

A full new upper floor, requires structural verification of the existing foundation and walls before design starts.

How the Process Runs

Every home addition follows the same three phases, in the same order, regardless of size.

Design, Zoning & Permits

Zoning and setback review, structural engineering, and permit drawings submitted before any construction is scheduled.

Foundation & Structural Shell

Foundation, framing, roofing, and exterior envelope built and weatherproofed before interior work begins.

Interior Build-Out & Finishes

Insulation, drywall, flooring, and finishes installed, then a final inspection and walkthrough.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Falling in love with a design before checking whether your lot's setbacks and zoning actually allow that footprint.
  • Matching new exterior materials so poorly the addition looks bolted-on instead of built as part of the house.
  • Undersizing the HVAC extension, which leaves the new space too hot in summer and too cold in winter.
  • Booking contractors before permits are approved, then paying for delays that were never the contractor's fault.
  • Committing to a second-storey addition before an engineer confirms the existing foundation and walls can carry the load.

When a Home Addition Isn't the Right Fit

An addition is a major project, and it's not always the answer, even when a house feels too small.

If the real problem is an awkward layout rather than a lack of square footage, reconfiguring what you already have through the design and planning service can solve it for a fraction of an addition's cost. If your lot's setbacks won't allow the footprint you want, a second-storey addition may be more realistic than building out, or vice versa; that's worth confirming before you commit to a design. And if your budget is under roughly $60,000, a small bump-out isn't realistic yet; a basement renovation often delivers more usable square footage per dollar in the meantime.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a home addition cost in the GTA?

Most GTA home additions run $60,000 to $450,000 depending on size and type. A small bump-out addition typically costs $60,000-$110,000. A single-storey rear or side addition runs $150,000-$280,000. A full second-storey addition can pass $250,000-$450,000.

Do I need a permit for a home addition?

Yes, always. Unlike smaller renovation projects, a home addition changes your home's footprint or structure, which requires a building permit, structural engineering drawings, and a zoning review in every GTA municipality.

How long does a home addition take from start to finish?

Design, engineering, and permit approval typically take 2-4 months. Construction runs another 4-8 months depending on size and whether it's a single-storey or second-storey addition. Permit timelines are usually the bigger variable, not the build itself.

Will my home addition need a zoning variance?

It depends on your lot's setback requirements and existing lot coverage. Narrower urban lots, corner lots, and older Toronto neighbourhoods are more likely to need a minor variance than larger suburban lots in York, Peel, or Durham Region.
