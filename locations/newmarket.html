Basement Planning in Newmarket

Newmarket's historic downtown core sits alongside real suburban growth, giving the town two very different renovation profiles depending on the block.

What Newmarket Says About Second Units

Every municipality writes its own rulebook for basement apartments, and Newmarket is no exception. It is not exciting reading, but it is far cheaper than finding out the hard way. Here is what the Town of Newmarket published on its own page when Reno Rise reviewed it (September 2026).

  • Newmarket asks for a Zoning Preliminary Review before the building permit application.
  • Registration became mandatory in 2013 under Bylaw 2013-13 (as amended) and requires fire department and Electrical Safety Authority documentation or a final permit inspection report.
  • Registered properties receive an “N” plate and the additional unit gets a “B” address designation, which helps emergency responders and waste collection tell the units apart.

Read the source before you plan: Town of Newmarket: additional or second unit information. Rules, fees and program deadlines change, so confirm with the municipality. Newmarket is outside the City of Toronto, so Toronto Building’s rules do not apply; the legal secondary suite guide explains the general considerations.

Whatever the age of the house, the plan starts the same way: measure the ceiling height, look for signs of moisture, and decide whether you want a finished basement or a self-contained suite. Get those answers early and the rest of the project gets calmer. See basement renovation planning, underpinning, waterproofing and egress windows for the most common issues.
